Hours that earn accrual:
Hours worked — always

No law sets this. Federal law does not require paid vacation at all, so what earns accrual is whatever your written policy says — and your own policy is what binds you. Counting all paid hours is the more generous, widely used choice; it stops someone accruing more slowly just because they took time off. Unticking everything gives the strict "hours actually worked" basis, which is the FLSA meaning of the term and the basis state paid-sick-leave minimums are measured on, so it never falls below them. One trap: if your "hours worked" column already includes paid leave, leave these unticked — otherwise those hours count twice.

Accrual is earned per hour actually worked, so a part-timer earns at the same rate as a full-timer — just on fewer hours.
